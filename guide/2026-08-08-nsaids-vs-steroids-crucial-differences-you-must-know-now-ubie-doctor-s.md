NSAIDs vs. Steroids: What's the Difference?

NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) and steroids both reduce inflammation and pain, but they work through different mechanisms and carry different risks.

NSAIDs (like ibuprofen, naproxen, and aspirin) block enzymes called COX-1 and COX-2 to reduce inflammation. Common side effects include stomach irritation, ulcers, and increased risk of heart or kidney issues with long-term use.

Steroids (like prednisone and cortisone) mimic hormones your body naturally produces to suppress the immune system and inflammation more broadly. Side effects can include weight gain, mood changes, high blood sugar, bone loss, and increased infection risk.

Key difference: NSAIDs are typically used for mild to moderate pain and inflammation, while steroids are prescribed for more severe or chronic inflammatory conditions.
