What are the primary causes of mood swings in females?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Mood swings in females are commonly caused by hormonal fluctuations—particularly shifts in estrogen levels tied to the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, perimenopause, and menopause. Physical activity, sleep quality, stress, and thyroid function can also influence mood stability. However, hormones aren't the only factor: mental health conditions, nutritional deficiencies, and certain medications may also contribute.

Because mood swings can stem from many overlapping causes, identifying the underlying trigger is the fastest path to relief. Explanation

Mood swings in females can be influenced by several factors. One major factor is hormonal changes, particularly involving estrogen. Estrogen plays a significant role in mood regulation, and fluctuations in its levels can lead to mood disorders. This is especially noticeable during periods of hormonal change, such as the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, or menopause.

Additionally, physical activity and the menstrual cycle itself can impact mood. Some studies suggest that changes in exercise routines or physical exertion levels can influence mood swings during different phases of the menstrual cycle. For instance, women may experience different mood patterns depending on their physical activity levels and the timing within their cycle.
