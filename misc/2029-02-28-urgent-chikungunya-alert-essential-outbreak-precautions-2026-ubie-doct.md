Internal Medicine

Chikungunya prevention focuses on avoiding mosquito bites, since the virus spreads through infected Aedes mosquitoes. Key steps include applying EPA-approved insect repellents (DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus), wearing long sleeves and pants, using window screens or bed nets, and eliminating standing water where mosquitoes breed. Peak biting hours are early morning and late afternoon, so limit outdoor exposure during these times.

If you've been bitten by mosquitoes and are experiencing fever, joint pain, rash, or fatigue, don't wait to find out what's going on. Symptoms of Chikungunya can mimic dengue, Zika, and other conditions, making self-diagnosis unreliable. Explanation

Chikungunya is a virus spread by mosquitoes. To stay safe during outbreaks, it is important to protect yourself from mosquito bites.

Use insect repellent: Apply repellent on exposed skin to keep mosquitoes away.

Wear protective clothing: Long-sleeved shirts and long pants can help cover your skin.

Stay indoors: Try to stay inside during times when mosquitoes are most active, especially early morning and late afternoon.

Use screens and nets: Ensure that windows and doors have screens to keep mosquitoes out. Sleeping under a mosquito net can also help.

These steps can reduce the chances of getting bitten by mosquitoes that may carry the Chikungunya virus. If you develop symptoms like fever, joint pain, or rash after being bitten, use our free AI symptom checker to help understand what might be causing your symptoms.
