What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a complex substance formed over centuries from the decomposition of plant material trapped between layers of rock. Key components include:

Fulvic acid: A potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory agent

Humic substances: Organic compounds that support mineral absorption

Trace minerals: Including iron, magnesium, zinc, and selenium

These constituents work together to influence cellular energy production, protect against oxidative stress, and support nutrient transport across cell membranes.

What the Research Says

While large-scale human trials are limited, several small studies and animal models provide insights:

Animal Studies

Rats given Shilajit showed improved memory and learning in maze tests.

Enhanced antioxidant enzyme activity was observed in the hippocampus.

Human Pilot Trials

In a small trial of healthy adults, daily Shilajit supplementation over 90 days improved self-reported mood and cognitive scores vs. placebo.

ADHD-Specific Research

Direct studies on Shilajit in ADHD populations are scarce.

Experts draw on data showing improved energy metabolism and antioxidant status to hypothesize potential benefits for ADHD focus levels.

Key takeaway: Current evidence is promising but preliminary. More rigorous, placebo-controlled trials in ADHD patients are needed to confirm efficacy and optimal dosing.

Shilajit and ADHD Focus Levels: What to Expect

Possible Benefits

Limitations

Not a substitute for prescribed ADHD medications (e.g., stimulants or non-stimulants)

Your doctor can help you weigh Shilajit's potential alongside evidence-based ADHD treatments and behavioral strategies.

Safety, Quality, and Dosage

Ensuring Quality

Shilajit supplements can be contaminated with heavy metals or adulterated with synthetic fillers. To minimize risk:

Choose third-party tested brands

Look for standardized fulvic acid content (>40%)

Avoid products with artificial additives or unknown sourcing

Possible Side Effects

Most people tolerate Shilajit well, but some may experience:

Mild stomach upset or diarrhea

Allergic reactions in sensitive individuals

Interactions with blood thinners or diabetes medications

Always start with a low dose (e.g., 300–500 mg per day of purified extract) and increase gradually under medical supervision.
