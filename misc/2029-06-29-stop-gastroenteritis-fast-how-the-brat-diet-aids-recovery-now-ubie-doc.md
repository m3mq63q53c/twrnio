The BRAT diet—bananas, rice, applesauce, and toast—is a short-term eating plan used to help manage diarrhea and mild stomach upset. These four bland, low-fiber foods are gentle on the digestive system, help firm up stools, and replace nutrients lost during illness. The BRAT diet is typically recommended for 24–48 hours, after which a normal, balanced diet should be resumed to ensure adequate nutrition.

Explanation

The BRAT diet is often recommended for people with gastroenteritis, which is an inflammation of the stomach and intestines often causing diarrhea. This diet consists of bananas, rice, applesauce, and toast. These foods are chosen because they are bland and easy to digest, which can help reduce stomach upset and diarrhea.

Benefits of the BRAT Diet:

Blandness: The foods in the BRAT diet are not spicy or heavy, which helps prevent further irritation to the stomach and intestines.

Low Fiber: These foods are low in fiber, which can help firm up stools and reduce diarrhea.

Nutrient Content: Bananas provide potassium, an important electrolyte that can be lost during diarrhea.

While the BRAT diet can be helpful in the short term, it is important to return to a balanced diet with a variety of nutrients as soon as possible to ensure proper nutrition and recovery.
