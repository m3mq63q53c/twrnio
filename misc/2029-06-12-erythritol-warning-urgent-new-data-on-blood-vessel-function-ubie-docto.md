How does erythritol affect blood vessel function?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Erythritol is a popular sugar substitute, but its effects on blood vessel health are debated. Some studies suggest erythritol may damage blood vessels by increasing the risk of clotting, inflammation, and cardiovascular events like heart attack or stroke. Other research indicates it has little to no significant impact on vascular function, especially at typical dietary levels.

Because the evidence is mixed, symptoms like unexplained fatigue, chest discomfort, headaches, or circulation issues shouldn't be brushed off—particularly if you regularly consume sugar substitutes. Explanation

Erythritol is a sweetener often used in foods for people who want to reduce their sugar intake. According to recent studies, its effects on blood vessels can vary. One study from 2023 suggests that erythritol might be linked to harmful cardiovascular events, which means it could possibly affect blood vessels negatively. However, another study from 2014 found that erythritol did not significantly change the function of blood vessels in patients.

Key Points

Potential Negative Effects: The 2023 study raises concerns about erythritol and its possible connection to cardiovascular events, suggesting it might harm blood vessels.
