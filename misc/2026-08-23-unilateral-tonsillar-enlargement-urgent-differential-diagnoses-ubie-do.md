What are the differential diagnoses for unilateral tonsillar enlargement?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Unilateral tonsillar enlargement—when one tonsil is noticeably larger than the other—can signal a range of underlying issues. Common causes include bacterial or viral infections (such as tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess, or mononucleosis), benign growths, and less commonly, malignancies like lymphoma or squamous cell carcinoma. Other contributors may include chronic inflammation, tonsil stones, or asymmetry from prior infections. Because the causes vary widely in severity, prompt evaluation by a healthcare provider is essential for accurate diagnosis and treatment.

Unilateral tonsillar enlargement means that one tonsil is bigger than the other. This can happen for several reasons:

Infections: Sometimes, a sore throat or other infections can cause one tonsil to swell more than the other.

Lymphoma: This is a type of cancer that can affect the tonsils, making one side grow larger.
