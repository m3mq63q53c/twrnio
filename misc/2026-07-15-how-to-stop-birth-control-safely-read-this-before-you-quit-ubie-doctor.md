How to stop taking birth control pills safely?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Stopping birth control pills is straightforward: finish your current pack, then don't start a new one. You can technically stop at any point, but completing the pack helps regulate your cycle's return. After discontinuing, expect your natural menstrual cycle to resume within 1-3 months, though timing varies by person. Some women experience temporary side effects like irregular periods, spotting, mood changes, or acne as hormone levels rebalance. Fertility typically returns quickly, so use alternative contraception if you're not planning pregnancy.

Because reactions to stopping birth control differ widely—and symptoms like prolonged irregular bleeding, severe cramping, or persistent hormonal changes can sometimes signal an underlying issue—it's worth understanding exactly what your body is telling you. Explanation

When you decide to stop taking birth control pills, it's generally safe to finish your current pack and then not start a new one. This approach helps your body adjust more smoothly. After stopping the pills, your body will begin to return to its natural cycle, but this can take some time.

Things to Expect:

Menstrual Changes: Your periods might not return right away. This is known as post-pill amenorrhea, and it can take a few months for your cycle to become regular again.

Hormonal Adjustments: You may experience some hormonal changes, such as mood swings or changes in your skin, as your body adjusts.

Tips:

Be Patient: It's normal for your body to take time to readjust.

Track Your Cycle: Keeping track of your menstrual cycle can help you understand how your body is adjusting.
