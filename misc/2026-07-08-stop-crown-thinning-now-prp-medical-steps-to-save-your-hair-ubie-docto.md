Why Is Your Crown Thinning?

Noticing your hair getting thinner at the crown can be unsettling, but it's a common issue many people face. Crown thinning often signals early stages of hair loss, and understanding the causes and options—like PRP for crown hair loss—can help you take control.

Understanding Crown Thinning

Hair grows in cycles:

Anagen (growth)

Catagen (transition)

Telogen (resting and shedding)

When this cycle is disrupted or when hair follicles shrink, new hairs become finer and shorter. Over time, that leads to visible thinning—especially at the crown, where follicles can be more sensitive to certain hormones.

Common Causes of Crown Thinning

The most frequent cause.

Linked to genetic sensitivity to dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

Pregnancy, menopause, thyroid disorders.

Severe stress or a high fever can push hair prematurely into the shedding phase (telogen effluvium).

Psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, fungal infections.

PRP for Crown Hair Loss

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy is gaining popularity as a minimally invasive option for improving hair density, including at the crown.

What Is PRP?

A sample of your blood is drawn and spun in a centrifuge.

Platelets (rich in growth factors) are concentrated and then injected into thinning scalp areas.

How PRP Works

Growth factors in platelets help wake up dormant hair follicles.

Encourages collagen production and increases blood supply to the scalp.

Over several months, new, thicker hairs can emerge.

Evidence and Effectiveness

Multiple small clinical studies show improved hair count and thickness.

Best results seen in early-stage thinning (like crown areas).

Results vary; some may need maintenance treatments every 4–6 months.

The PRP Procedure

Session length: 45–60 minutes

Frequency: 3–4 sessions, once a month

Downtime: Minimal; mild soreness or redness for a day

Risks: Infection (rare), temporary scalp tenderness

Other Medically Approved Next Steps

If PRP alone isn't enough or you're looking for complementary treatments, consider these options:

Topical Minoxidil

Applied once or twice daily.

Stimulates hair follicles and prolongs the growth phase.

Side effects: scalp irritation, initial shedding.

Oral Finasteride (for men)

Blocks DHT formation.

Can stabilize or reverse thinning at the crown.

Side effects: sexual dysfunction (rare), mood changes.

Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT)

Hair Transplantation

For advanced crown baldness.

Follicular unit extraction (FUE) or strip harvesting.

Permanent but more invasive and costly.

Rapid hair shedding (clumps of hair in your brush).

Scalp symptoms: severe itching, redness, oozing.
