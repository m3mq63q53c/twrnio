Mounjaro’s Role in Weight Loss

Mounjaro, also known as tirzepatide, is a self-injectable medication intended to help manage Type 2 diabetes. You need a prescription to obtain it. This medicine works by targeting two important hormones in your body: GLP-1, which helps lower blood sugar and makes you feel full, and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), which helps release insulin.

Now, it is important to state that Mounjaro is specifically meant to treat diabetes, not to help people lose weight. Although it may help people with obesity shed pounds, it has not yet been officially approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for weight loss purposes. On the other hand, Zepbound, another form of tirzepatide, has been approved by the FDA for weight loss.

In one study of over 2,500 obese adults, those who took 5 milligrams of tirzepatide for about a year and a half lost an average of 15% of their body weight.

That study determined that higher doses seemed to lead to even more weight loss. Because of these results, some doctors are prescribing Mounjaro “off-label” to help people with obesity, even if they don’t have diabetes. This means they are using it for a purpose other than what it was originally approved for. This is not unheard of, as using medications off-label is quite common and considered acceptable by the FDA when it is deemed medically appropriate.
