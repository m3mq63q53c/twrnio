Internal Medicine

Restless leg syndrome (RLS) is a common condition during pregnancy, affecting up to 1 in 5 expecting mothers. It causes uncomfortable sensations and an urge to move the legs, often disrupting sleep. Fortunately, RLS in pregnancy can be managed safely through lifestyle adjustments, gentle stretching, iron or folate supplementation (when recommended by a doctor), and improved sleep hygiene.

Because RLS symptoms can overlap with other conditions—such as anemia, circulation issues, or nerve problems—it's important to understand what may be driving your discomfort before deciding on next steps. How RLS Affects Pregnancy

[Pregnancy The](https://satietybrief.china-akan.com/blog/4003772064.html)

Prevalence: RLS is quite common in pregnant women, especially during the third trimester.

Sleep Disruption: It can lead to poor sleep because of the uncomfortable sensations in the legs.

Impact on Daily Life: Lack of sleep can make you feel tired and affect your daily activities.

Management of RLS in Pregnancy

Lifestyle Changes: Simple changes like regular exercise, stretching, and a healthy diet can help manage symptoms.

Medical Guidance: It's important to talk to a healthcare provider for safe treatment options during pregnancy.
