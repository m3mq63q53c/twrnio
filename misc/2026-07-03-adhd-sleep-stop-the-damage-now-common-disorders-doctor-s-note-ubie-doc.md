How does ADHD affect sleep and what sleep disorders are common with ADHD?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

ADHD and sleep problems are closely linked. Adults and children with ADHD frequently experience sleep disorders such as insomnia, restless legs syndrome, delayed sleep phase syndrome, and sleep apnea. Racing thoughts, difficulty "switching off," and disrupted circadian rhythms can make falling and staying asleep especially challenging.

People with ADHD often have trouble sleeping. They might find it hard to fall asleep or stay asleep.

Common Sleep Disorders in ADHD:

Insomnia: This means having trouble falling asleep or staying asleep.

Restless Legs Syndrome: This is when you feel like you have to move your legs, especially at night.

Sleep Apnea: This is when you stop breathing for a short time while sleeping.

[Note Ubie Doctor's Note](https://leanorbit.dns0663.com/blog/9059641824.html)

These sleep problems can make ADHD symptoms worse. When you don't sleep well, it can be harder to pay attention and control your actions during the day. If you or someone you know is having trouble with sleep and ADHD, it might help to talk to a doctor or a sleep specialist.
