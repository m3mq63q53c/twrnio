What is CBD?

CBD is short for cannabidiol, a chemical compound that’s found in Cannabis sativa plants. That includes both hemp and marijuana plants, which are technically different. Hemp is a cannabis plant with less than 0.3% THC, whereas marijuana contains at least 0.3% THC.30 While not part of the classification system, the inverse remains true for CBD; hemp contains far more CBD than THC. That’s why most CBD is derived from hemp plants.

Inside your body, CBD acts on several systems, many in the brain. It’s an agonist for serotonin receptors, which helps explain why it makes you feel good and reduces anxiety.9 It also has a role in preventing the breakdown of anandamide, a lipid mediator that acts on CB1 and CB2 cannabinoid receptors to make them more sensitive to endogenous cannabinoids.10 And since endocannabinoid signaling is part of the neuromodulatory system, some researchers point out that increased anandamide can fight anxiety.11

With so many potential mechanisms of action involved, some overlapping to enhance CBD’s effects, mapping out its exact activity in fighting anxiety remains elusive. However, plenty of research has concluded that CBD can fight anxiety and modulate pain.9

Full vs. broad spectrum

Cannabis plants — including both hemp and marijuana plants — contain more than 100 cannabinoids in them, only one of which is cannabidiol, or CBD.19 Through the processes of plant selection and extraction, manufacturers can control (to an extent) which of these cannabinoids makes it into a product.

You’ll sometimes see CBD isolate offered as a guaranteed THC-free CBD, but there are a few very important reasons to choose either broad-spectrum or full-spectrum CBD instead. In a nutshell, the specific characteristics of broad-spectrum and full-spectrum are:

Full-spectrum CBD

Full-spectrum extracts contain all of the naturally occurring cannabinoids, terpenes, and other compounds in the cannabis plant. This includes both CBD and Delta-9 THC, the compound most closely associated with marijuana’s psychotropic effects. However, CBD is typically produced from hemp plants, which contain less than 0.3% THC, leaving only trace amounts in the average full-spectrum product. And that menagerie of hemp’s various compounds creates something we’ll discuss below: the entourage effect.

Broad-spectrum CBD

Broad-spectrum CBD utilizes a refined extraction process that can prevent THC from occurring at any measurable amount in a CBD product. Some broad-spectrum products still contain trace THC, and they'll say as much on the label. However, companies that eliminate it completely tend to advertise it loudly, as some consumers might want to avoid THC for stigma concerns, upcoming drug tests, or other personal reasons. Much of the aforementioned entourage effect is still present in broad-spectrum CBD, but the specific effect when CBD and THC are combined is missing.

There is some evidence that marijuana users have superior erectile performance to non-users, which lends some credence to the argument in favor of full-spectrum extracts or even CBD products with elevated levels of THC in them.23

To recap quickly, CBD is a non-psychoactive component of cannabis plants (both hemp and marijuana) that offers various benefits to humans, including pain management and anti-anxiety effects. And THC is the psychoactive component of those same plants, which offers similar benefits alongside its mind-altering abilities.

What is the entourage effect?

In studies looking at CBD in isolation, its anti-anxiety effects were present but more often required significant doses compared to what you see on the market.3 But there’s no shortage of anecdotal evidence that many of these low-dose products have some efficacy when it comes to anxiety and pain management. But how can 25mg of CBD outperform 300mg of CBD used in a study?

The answer lies in what’s been dubbed the entourage effect.20 CBD and THC have garnered a lot of attention for their efficacy, which has been studied and characterized in dozens, if not hundreds, of studies over the years. But of the more than 100 other cannabinoids in cannabis plants, many offer similar benefits. The only problem is that studies into things like CBG, CBC, and CBN are few and far between. On their own, those studies show some promise, but these individual components don’t perform as well on their own as CBD and THC do.21

However, when studies provide participants with more complex CBD formulations that include a full spectrum of cannabinoids, including THC, CBG, CBN, CBC, and all their brothers and sisters, effects typically associated with CBD become more pronounced at lower doses.22 This is the entourage effect in action: When you combine CBD with THC and other effective cannabis constituents, you create an effect that’s greater than the sum of its parts.

FAQ about CBD gummies for ED

How can a CBD gummy help with erectile dysfunction?

While there isn’t much evidence that CBD can improve circulation or nitric oxide expression (two key behaviors of many ED supplements), there is plenty of evidence that it can quell stress and anxiety.2 If your ED has to do with stress or anxiety, or those issues arise because of your ED and make the situation worse, CBD could help.3

Will a CBD gummy make me fail a drug test?

It can. CBD gummies contain varying amounts of THC, depending on how they're processed. Full-spectrum CBD contains the most, broad-spectrum often contains too little for a test to detect (but not always), and isolate is wholly THC-free.

Does CBD come from marijuana?

Not in the way you mean. CBD and THC are both cannabinoids, but THC is the psychoactive compound associated with marijuana. Both appear in cannabis plants, but the marijuana plant has far more THC than CBD, and the hemp plant has far more CBD than THC.

How is it legal to ship THC products at all, let alone across state lines?

According to a provision in the 2018 Federal Farm Bill, hemp-derived CBD products containing less than 0.3% THC by weight are legal to ship around the country. That said, as long as a gummy is heavy enough, it can contain a pretty high dose of THC, which will show up on drug tests.
