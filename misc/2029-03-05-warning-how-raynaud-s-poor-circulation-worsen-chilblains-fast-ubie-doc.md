Poor circulation and Raynaud's syndrome can worsen chilblains by restricting healthy blood flow to the skin. When blood vessels narrow or spasm in response to cold, the small vessels in fingers, toes, ears, and nose struggle to rewarm properly. This delayed reaction triggers inflammation, swelling, itching, and the red or purple patches characteristic of chilblains. People with Raynaud's are especially prone to repeated flare-ups during cold, damp weather.

Because chilblains can overlap with other circulation-related conditions, understanding your specific symptoms is key to finding relief and avoiding complications.
