Explanation

Diabetes can quietly damage nerves and blood flow in your feet, leading to pain, ulcerations and, in severe cases, amputation. Custom diabetic orthotics—shoe inserts tailored to your feet—aim to reduce pressure points, improve alignment and protect against complications. But they can be expensive. In this guide, we break down what custom diabetic orthotics are, how much they cost, insurance coverage options and whether they deliver enough value to justify the investment.

What Are Custom Diabetic Orthotics?

Custom diabetic orthotics are specialized shoe inserts designed to fit your feet exactly. They differ from over-the-counter (OTC) inserts in three key ways:

Precision fitMade from a 3D scan or cast of your foot, ensuring support where you need it most.

Medical-grade materialsOften use cushioning foams, plastics or carbon fiber to off-load pressure.

Clinician oversightPrescribed and adjusted by a podiatrist, orthotist or other qualified professional.

Why They Matter for Diabetes

Reduced risk of ulcersEven minor pressure spots can break down skin in someone with diabetic neuropathy.

Improved gait and balanceCorrecting foot alignment helps prevent falls.

Pain reliefTargeted support can ease plantar fasciitis, metatarsalgia and other aches.

Insurance Coverage Guide

Many insurance plans recognize the medical necessity of custom diabetic orthotics. Here's how to navigate coverage:

Medicare (Part B)

Under Medicare Part B, you may qualify for one pair of extra-depth shoes and up to three sets of custom-molded inserts per calendar year, if you have diabetes and meet one of these conditions:

Previous amputation of foot/toes

History of foot ulceration

Pre-ulcerative calluses

Peripheral neuropathy with evidence of callus formation

Foot deformity

Poor circulation

Private Insurance

Coverage can vary widely. Key steps:

Review your plan's durable medical equipment (DME) benefits

Check for any annual limits or co-pays on orthotics

Ask if your insurer requires pre-authorization or second opinions

Submit supporting medical records (e.g., diagnosis of neuropathy, prior ulcers)

Many plans cover 50–100% of the cost once you meet a deductible. FSA/HSA dollars are also eligible.

Medicaid

Medicaid coverage depends on state rules. Some states cover custom orthotics for diabetes under DME; others limit to emergency cases. Contact your local Medicaid office or benefits coordinator.

Are Custom Diabetic Orthotics Worth the Price?

To decide if custom orthotics are right for you, weigh the up-front cost against the potential savings and health benefits:

Preventing Costly ComplicationsA single diabetic foot ulcer can cost thousands in wound care, infection treatment and hospitalization.

Alternatives to Custom Orthotics

If cost or coverage is a barrier, consider these options:

Prefabricated diabetic insolesLess expensive ($20–$100), designed with extra cushioning and arch support.

Semi-custom orthoticsOff-the-shelf shells you trim at home or have adjusted by a shoe store.

Extra-depth diabetic shoesRoomier footwear that accommodates thicker insoles and reduces pressure.

Lifestyle and footwear changesDaily foot inspections, moisture-wicking socks and supportive athletic shoes.

While not as precise, these alternatives can still offer significant relief and protection.

Tips for Maximizing Insurance Benefits

Obtain a clear diagnosis and document any history of ulcers, deformities or neuropathy.

Ask your provider to submit a detailed "Letter of Medical Necessity."

Verify network status: using an in-network orthotist or supplier often reduces your co-pay.

Keep copies of all paperwork—prescriptions, receipts and provider notes.

If denied, appeal promptly with additional medical records and a physician's endorsement.

Next Steps

Schedule a foot exam with your podiatrist or orthotist.

Gather your insurance benefit details (DME coverage, deductibles, in-network providers).

Discuss whether custom orthotics or a less expensive alternative fits your risk profile.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Custom diabetic orthotics can significantly reduce your risk of serious foot complications, but they're not a substitute for regular medical care. If you notice:

New numbness or tingling in your feet

Redness, swelling or drainage from an area on your foot

Sudden increase in foot or ankle pain

Any sign of infection (warmth, fever, red streaks)

—speak to a doctor immediately. Life-threatening complications can develop quickly in people with diabetes.

Conclusion

"Custom diabetic inserts cost" more upfront than OTC options, but for many people with diabetes, they're a smart investment. By preventing ulcers, reducing pain and improving mobility, properly fitted custom orthotics can help you maintain an active life while avoiding costly medical complications. Check your insurance benefits, discuss options with your healthcare team and consider a risk-based approach: sometimes the most expensive choice today can save thousands down the road. Above all, stay proactive in monitoring your foot health and speak to a doctor about any serious or rapidly changing symptoms.
