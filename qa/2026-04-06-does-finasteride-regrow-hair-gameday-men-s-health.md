What Is Finasteride?

Finasteride is an oral medication commonly prescribed to treat male pattern hair loss, also known as androgenetic alopecia. It is typically taken once daily and works from within the body rather than being applied to the scalp.

The key factor behind male pattern hair loss is a hormone called dihydrotestosterone, or DHT. This hormone is derived from testosterone and plays a role in shrinking hair follicles over time. As follicles become smaller, they produce thinner, weaker hairs until growth eventually stops. (2)

Finasteride addresses this issue by targeting the hormonal pathway responsible for DHT production. Specifically, it inhibits an enzyme known as 5-alpha reductase, which converts testosterone into DHT. By lowering DHT levels, finasteride helps protect hair follicles from further damage. (3)

Because it works systemically, finasteride is often used as a long-term treatment for men looking to slow or manage ongoing hair loss.

How Finasteride Works to Treat Hair Loss

To understand how finasteride works, it helps to look more closely at the role of DHT in hair loss.

DHT binds to receptors in hair follicles, particularly in areas like the hairline and crown. In men who are genetically sensitive to this hormone, DHT gradually shrinks the follicles in a process called miniaturization. Over time, this leads to shorter, finer hairs and eventually stops growth altogether. (4)

Finasteride reduces the amount of DHT circulating in the body and in the scalp. With less DHT available, hair follicles are less likely to undergo miniaturization. This creates a more stable environment for hair growth.

Rather than directly stimulating new growth, finasteride helps maintain follicle health and slows the overall progression of hair loss. It can also help extend the growth phase of the hair cycle, allowing existing hairs to grow longer and thicker.

This distinction is important for men to understand before starting treatment. Finasteride is not a quick fix or a direct growth stimulant. Instead, it works behind the scenes to address the root cause of hair loss in many men.

Does Finasteride Actually Regrow Hair?

So, does finasteride actually regrow hair? In some cases, the answer may be yes. However, that is not the medication’s primary function.

Many men who take finasteride experience stabilization of their hair loss. This means that shedding slows down or stops, and their existing hair is preserved. For a significant number of users, this may be considered a successful outcome, as it substantially slows down hair loss.

In addition to slowing hair loss, some men may also notice regrowth. This is more likely to occur in areas where hair follicles are still active but weakened, rather than in areas that are completely bald. (5) Regrowth often appears as thicker, fuller hair rather than entirely new strands in previously bare spots.

Several factors influence whether regrowth occurs, including how early treatment begins and how responsive an individual is to the medication.

What Factors Influence Finasteride Results?

Not all men experience the same results with finasteride. Several factors can influence how effective the treatment is, and understanding these can help set more personalized expectations.

Stage of hair loss: Men in earlier stages tend to see better outcomes because more follicles are still active

Age: Younger men may respond more quickly, though finasteride can be effective at various ages

Genetics: Individual sensitivity to DHT and overall hair growth patterns play a significant role

Consistency of treatment: Taking the medication daily as prescribed is critical for maintaining the best possible results

It’s also important to consider that hair loss is a progressive condition. Even with treatment, the goal is often to slow or manage the process rather than completely reverse it. What Happens If You Stop Taking Finasteride?

Finasteride works by continuously reducing DHT levels. If you stop taking the medication, those levels will gradually return to baseline.

When this happens, the underlying process of hair loss resumes, and over time, hair that was improved during treatment may begin to thin again.

This doesn’t happen overnight, but changes can become noticeable within a few months of discontinuing the medication. The timeline varies depending on the individual and how long they were on treatment, as well as what results they experienced.

It’s important to think of finasteride as an ongoing commitment rather than a short-term solution. Maintaining results typically requires continued use. Combining Finasteride With Other Hair Loss Treatments

Because finasteride targets the hormonal cause of hair loss, it is often used alongside other treatments that focus on different aspects of hair growth.

One common approach is combining finasteride with topical treatments like minoxidil. While finasteride helps reduce DHT and protect follicles, minoxidil works at the scalp level to stimulate growth and improve hair thickness.

This combination can provide a more comprehensive approach by addressing both the cause and the visible effects of hair loss.
