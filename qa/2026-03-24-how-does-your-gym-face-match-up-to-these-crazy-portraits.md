# ​How Does Your ‘Gym Face’ Match Up To These Crazy Portraits?

Your body reacts in all sorts of strange and complicated ways when you’re lifting heavy—and your face is no exception.

The “gym face” is never really pleasant to look at or experience: Your forehead is dripping sweat, your mouth is pulled into a tight grimace, lips snarling, as if you’re ready to implode.

[To These](https://reboundworks.escortskhi.com/blog/7708726507.html)

So naturally, an artist in California decided to take your ugly gym face and transform it into art, reports the Los Angeles Times. Chris Finely’s exhibit of seven paintings titled “Drool, Snatch, Clean and Jerk” in Chimento Contemporary art gallery in Los Angeles zooms in on weightlifters as they perform the snatch, in which you would lift your barbell overhead from the ground in one continuous move, and the clean and jerk, which involves lifting your barbell from the floor, to your shoulders, and then overhead.

The paintings are crazy in the best way, cartoon-like and candid. And the longer we looked at them, the more we wondered: Do we really look like that when we’re pumping iron?

[These Crazy](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/)

Well, probably—but it’s actually a good thing.

Believe it or not, your ugly gym face is your body’s way of trying to make you stronger during difficult exercises, like the overhead press, pullup, and lateral raise, explains Bill Hartman, P.T., C.S.C.S., owner of Indianapolis Sports and Fitness Training. (Here’s why every man should be lifting weights.)

Think of it this way: Your head and neck create one sturdy column, and the stronger it is, the more efficiently your arm and shoulder muscles can lift and lower the weight around it, Hartman says. As you perform these upper-body movements, muscles like your traps and delts actually tug at your head and neck, weakening your column. So your body reacts to stop the pulling, causing your jaw to shift.

That’s because your bottom and top jaws are actually misaligned, he explains, but it’s not really visible to the naked eye. However, your brain senses the asymmetry and tries to correct it by temporarily aligning your bottom jaw to the top, forcing the muscles around your mouth to shift. Before you know it, you’re snarling, and probably look like one of Finley’s paintings.

[These Crazy](https://thymicshield.dns0663.com/)

Funny thing is, we bet you can’t stay expressionless while trying to lift heavy. Try it for yourself, and you might notice that you can’t lift the weight. Then, let your face do its thing, just like these paintings. “You’ll likely be able to pick it up,” says Hartman.
