Internal Medicine

Menstrual bleeding that stops and starts again can occur for several reasons. Often, it reflects normal hormonal fluctuations during your cycle, especially shifts in estrogen and progesterone levels. Other common causes include stress, birth control changes, thyroid issues, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), uterine fibroids, endometriosis, or early pregnancy. In some cases, start-and-stop bleeding may signal an underlying condition that benefits from medical evaluation.

Explanation

Your menstrual cycle can sometimes be unpredictable. When your period stops and then starts again, it can happen because of changes in the hormones that control your cycle. Hormonal imbalances can lead to irregular bleeding. This is sometimes seen in a condition called oligomenorrhea, where menstrual periods are less frequent than usual. In some cases, the bleeding might be a normal reaction as your body adjusts to stress, weight changes, or new medications like birth control. However, in early pregnancy, some women may also experience bleeding that may seem like a return of their period, which is something to be aware of. Additionally, issues with the uterus or other reproductive parts can sometimes cause abnormal vaginal bleeding. While these changes might be normal for some women, if you notice heavy bleeding, severe pain, or if the irregularity continues, it is important to talk with a healthcare provider.
