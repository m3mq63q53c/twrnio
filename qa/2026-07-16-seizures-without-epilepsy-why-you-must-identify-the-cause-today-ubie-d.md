Internal Medicine

Seizures can occur without epilepsy. A single seizure does not automatically mean a person has epilepsy. In fact, many people experience one seizure in their lifetime without ever having another. These are known as provoked seizures and are often triggered by identifiable causes such as high fever, low blood sugar, dehydration, head injury, alcohol or drug withdrawal, certain medications, or lack of sleep. Epilepsy, by contrast, is diagnosed when a person has two or more unprovoked seizures occurring more than 24 hours apart.

Because seizures can stem from many different underlying conditions, understanding the cause is critical to determining next steps. Whether you or a loved one recently experienced a seizure-like episode, identifying possible triggers early can help guide the right conversation with a healthcare provider. Febrile seizures: Seizures triggered by fever, common in young children. Head injuries: Trauma can cause a one-time seizure.
