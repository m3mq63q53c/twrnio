Ankle tightness or a squeezing sensation is often caused by musculoskeletal conditions, osteochondral defects, or cuboid syndrome — issues that affect the bones, cartilage, and soft tissues of the foot and ankle. Common contributing factors include prior sprains, repetitive stress, joint instability, or misalignment of the small bones in the midfoot.

Explanation

The feeling of your ankle being squeezed can be caused by several conditions affecting the foot and ankle.

Musculoskeletal Conditions: According to medical literature, various musculoskeletal conditions can impact the foot and ankle. These conditions may lead to discomfort, pain, or a squeezing sensation due to inflammation or structural issues in the bones and tissues.

Osteochondral Defects: These are problems with the bone and cartilage in the ankle joint. They can cause pain and a feeling of pressure or squeezing, especially when the joint is in motion or bearing weight.

Cuboid Syndrome: This condition involves the cuboid bone in the foot. When this bone is out of alignment, it can cause a sensation of squeezing or discomfort in the ankle area.

Each of these conditions can affect how the foot and ankle feel, especially during movement or weight-bearing activities.
