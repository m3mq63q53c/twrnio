Why do I feel a lump in my stomach when sitting up?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Feeling a lump in your stomach when sitting up is often caused by a hernia, where tissue pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal wall and becomes more visible with movement. Other possible causes include benign growths (like lipomas or cysts), diastasis recti (separation of abdominal muscles), an enlarged organ, or swollen lymph nodes. Because the lump becomes more noticeable with body position, it typically signals a structural change rather than something temporary.

Since abdominal lumps can range from harmless to serious, identifying the likely cause early helps you decide whether to monitor at home or seek medical care. Explanation

When you sit up and feel a lump in your stomach, it could be because certain structures shift or become more obvious when you change position. One possible explanation is a hernia, where part of your internal tissue pushes through a weak spot in your abdominal wall. Sometimes, benign growths such as lipomas (fatty tumors) or cysts can also present as a lump that is more noticeable when your abdominal muscles are engaged. In rare cases, more unusual conditions like a gastric volvulus, where your stomach twists on itself, might cause symptoms including a palpable mass, although this is less common and associated with pain and vomiting. Since there are several possible reasons for this sensation, it's important to have it evaluated by a healthcare provider. You can start by taking a free symptom checker to help identify potential causes before your appointment. They can perform a physical examination and may order imaging tests to accurately determine what is causing the lump and recommend the proper treatment if needed.
