What Are Skin Tags?

Skin tags (acrochordons) are benign, soft pieces of skin that hang off the body by a small stalk. They're usually:

Flesh-colored or slightly darker

Painless and slow-growing

Common in adults, especially with age, obesity, or diabetes

Found in places with friction or moisture

Although skin tags themselves aren't dangerous, it's important to confirm the diagnosis. Some skin lesions that look like tags can be other conditions—rarely, even skin cancer.

Risks and Limitations of ACV for Skin Tags Removal

Although ACV can irritate skin cells, there's no reliable scientific evidence proving it fully or safely removes skin tags. Potential problems include:

• Chemical burns and severe irritation• Redness, itching or blistering• Pain and prolonged healing time• Infection if skin barrier is broken• Scarring and lasting discoloration

Beyond local side effects, you may also:

• Mistreat a lesion that isn't a skin tag (for example, a wart or melanoma)• Delay proper care for a more serious condition

No major dermatology associations or peer-reviewed studies endorse ACV for skin tag removal. In contrast, board-certified dermatologists and family doctors have access to proven, controlled methods.
