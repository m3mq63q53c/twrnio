Feeling "Off" on Gabapentin? The Reality & Medical Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Gabapentin side effects like drowsiness, dizziness, brain fog, and balance problems are common and usually improve within 1–3 weeks. However, severe confusion, trouble breathing, facial swelling, rash, or suicidal thoughts are medical emergencies. Never stop gabapentin abruptly.

Key factors affecting how you feel include recent dose changes, combining gabapentin with alcohol or other sedating medications, older age, and kidney disease. Your doctor can help with timing adjustments, safe tapering, and alternatives.

Because gabapentin symptoms overlap with many other conditions—and because dose, age, and kidney function all matter—it's worth clarifying what's actually driving how you feel before your next appointment. Explanation

If you've started gabapentin and don't quite feel like yourself, you're not alone. Many people report feeling "off" in the first days or weeks. Gabapentin is commonly prescribed for nerve pain, seizures, restless legs syndrome, and sometimes anxiety or hot flashes. While it can be very effective, the side effects of gabapentin are real — and understanding them can help you decide what to do next.

Let's walk through what's normal, what's not, and when it's time to talk to a doctor.

What Is Gabapentin and Why Can It Make You Feel Different?

Gabapentin works by calming overactive nerve signals in the brain and body. That calming effect is helpful for pain and seizures — but it can also affect how alert, steady, or emotionally balanced you feel.

Because it acts on the nervous system, some people experience mental or physical changes, especially:

When first starting the medication

After a dose increase

If taking higher doses

If combined with other sedating medications

Most side effects are mild and improve over time. But some require medical attention.

Common Side Effects of Gabapentin

The most frequently reported side effects of gabapentin are related to the brain and nervous system. These often improve within a few weeks as your body adjusts.

Common (Usually Mild to Moderate)

Drowsiness or fatigue

Dizziness

Feeling "foggy" or mentally slow

Unsteadiness or balance problems

Blurred vision

Mild swelling in the hands or feet

When Feeling "Off" Might Be Something More Serious

Most side effects of gabapentin are manageable. However, certain symptoms are not normal and should be evaluated promptly.

Seek medical attention right away if you experience:

Trouble breathing

Severe drowsiness that's hard to wake from

Confusion or extreme disorientation

Swelling of the face, lips, or throat

Severe rash or blistering skin

Sudden mood changes with suicidal thoughts

These could indicate a serious allergic reaction or medication complication.

Who Is More Likely to Feel Side Effects?

Some people are more sensitive to gabapentin.

Higher risk groups include:

Older adults

People with kidney disease

Those taking opioids, benzodiazepines, or alcohol

Individuals on multiple sedating medications

Gabapentin is cleared through the kidneys. If kidney function is reduced, the drug can build up in the body and increase side effects. This is why dose adjustments are often necessary.

How Long Do Side Effects of Gabapentin Last?

For many people:

Mild drowsiness or dizziness improves within 1–3 weeks.

Cognitive "fog" may lessen as your body adjusts.

Swelling or weight gain may persist if the medication is continued.

If symptoms last beyond a few weeks or interfere with daily life, it's reasonable to reassess the dose or consider alternatives.

Never stop gabapentin suddenly without medical supervision. Abruptly stopping can cause:

Withdrawal symptoms

Insomnia

Sweating

Seizures (especially if used for seizure control)

If discontinuation is needed, doctors typically taper the dose gradually.

Practical Steps If You're Feeling "Off"

If you suspect the side effects of gabapentin are affecting you, here's what you can do:

1. Track Your Symptoms

Write down:

When symptoms started

Your current dose

Any recent dose changes

Other medications you're taking

Patterns can help your doctor make safe adjustments.

2. Avoid Alcohol and Sedatives

Alcohol and certain medications can significantly increase:

Drowsiness

Breathing suppression

Confusion

Combining these can be dangerous.

3. Adjust Timing (With Medical Approval)

Some people benefit from:

Taking larger doses at night

Dividing doses differently

Always check with your healthcare provider before making changes.

4. Check for Other Causes

Feeling "off" isn't always from gabapentin. It could also be:

Low blood sugar

Thyroid imbalance

Dehydration

Sleep problems

Depression

Should You Stop Gabapentin?

[Doctor's Note](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/)

Do not stop gabapentin abruptly unless instructed by a healthcare professional.

Sudden discontinuation can cause:

Rebound nerve pain

Increased seizure risk

Withdrawal-like symptoms

If side effects are intolerable, your doctor may:

Reduce the dose

Switch to a similar medication (like pregabalin)

Try a completely different treatment

Adjust other medications contributing to symptoms

There are options — you are not "stuck" with feeling unwell.

Balancing Benefits vs. Side Effects

It's important to weigh:

How much gabapentin is helping

How much the side effects are interfering

For example:

If nerve pain has significantly improved but mild drowsiness remains manageable, continuing may make sense.

If cognitive fog is affecting work or safety (like driving), reassessment is appropriate.

Medication decisions should always be individualized.

Special Considerations for Older Adults

Older adults are more sensitive to the side effects of gabapentin, particularly:

Falls

Sedation

If you or a loved one has experienced falls or sudden mental changes after starting gabapentin, speak to a doctor promptly. Dose adjustments are often needed.

When to Speak to a Doctor Immediately

Contact a healthcare professional urgently if you experience:

Thoughts of self-harm

Severe confusion

Difficulty breathing

Severe allergic reaction symptoms

Extreme drowsiness or inability to stay awake

These could be life-threatening and require immediate medical care.

Feeling "off" on gabapentin is not uncommon. The most typical side effects of gabapentin include:

Balance issues
