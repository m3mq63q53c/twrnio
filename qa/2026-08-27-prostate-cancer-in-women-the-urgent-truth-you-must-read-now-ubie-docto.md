Is it possible for women to develop prostate cancer?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Can women get prostate cancer? Technically, women do not have a prostate gland, but they do have Skene's glands—often called the "female prostate"—located near the urethra. In rare cases, cancer can develop in these glands, resulting in a condition similar to prostate cancer in men. Symptoms may include urinary changes, pelvic pain, or unusual bleeding.

Because these symptoms overlap with many other common conditions, it can be difficult to know what's actually causing them. Getting clarity early matters—especially with rare cancers, where timely evaluation can meaningfully impact outcomes. Transgender Women: Transgender women, especially those who have undergone hormone therapy, might also be at risk for prostate cancer. This is because they typically retain their prostate gland even after gender-affirming surgeries.
