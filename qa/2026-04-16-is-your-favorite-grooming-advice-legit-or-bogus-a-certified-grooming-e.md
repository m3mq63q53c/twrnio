# Is Your Favorite Grooming Advice Legit or Bogus? A Certified Grooming Expert Weighs In

The Men’s Health style team knows that a little bit of game-changing grooming advice can go a long way. But it always amazes us how much bogus advice is out there.

So we decided to go down the halls of the Men’s Health offices to ask the editorial team for the best grooming advice they’ve ever heard. You probably follow some of these tips yourself.

Related: The Men’s Health Big Book Of Uncommon Knowledge—thousands of DIY tips, how-to articles, and awesome skills every modern man must master

Now, these editors are not grooming experts—just average guys who’ve found something that works. Luckily, the style team happens to know a few pros.

We brought in Kiehl’s president and certified grooming expert Christ Salgardo to review the editors’ favorite tips and find out who knows his stuff and who’s better off letting the style team handle the grooming advice from here on out.

1. Manscape Without Worry

Dan Michel, Fashion Editor

“When manscaping sensitive areas, use a shave oil instead of cream or foam so you can see what you’re doing.”

Salgardo’s verdict: Yay.

“Definitely. Use something translucent, like an oil or gel.”

Try American Crew Ultra Gliding Shave Oil ($10, loxabeauty.com).

2 . Prep Your Pores Before You Shave

Josh Feigenbaum, Integrated Marketing Manager

“The best advice I have ever received is to shave right after a hot shower. The hot water and steam help open your pores and make the hair softer. Not only does it make it easier to shave but it also reduces irritation.”

[or Bogus Certified Grooming](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/4401725759.html)

But FYI… “You can also shave after a shower—or get the same effect with a hot wet wash cloth. Place it on your face for a minute or two before shaving to open up your pores.”

3. Stay Sharp With a Quality Electric Trimmer

Tom O’Quinn, Creative Director

“The best advice I ever got was that it’s worth the money to buy a really good beard trimmer, and keep your neck clean below the Adam’s apple. A beard trimmer that’s waterproof and works in the shower is great, because the mess is easier to clean up!”

“A trimmer is a great investment. It will keep you looking good in between trips to the barber.”

We recommend The Crafter Beard Boss Style and Detail Kit by Remington ($36, amazon.com).

4. You Don’t Have To Be a Genius To Look Great

[Weighs In](https://thymicshield.dns0663.com/)

Andrew Daniels, Senior Online Editor

“My wife told me to stop wearing my hair like Jimmy Neutron.”

Shutterstock Salgardo’s verdict: Nay!

“A big hair shape can add fullness, but it doesn’t work for all face shapes. Make sure to check with your stylist.”

5. Buddy Up With Your Barber

Dean Stattmann, Deputy Editor, Digital Projects

“Don’t get your hair cut at the mall. Find a good barber and never leave him! Every haircut shouldn’t be a gamble. Staying loyal to one barber who knows what you want will make your life so much easier. It’s the best way to keep your style consistent, and after a few cuts you’ll be one of those guys who can sit down and ask for ‘the usual.’ As a bonus, you’ll have better conversation—no more painful small talk. Just don’t forget to tip.”

Better yet… “Get a stylist and a barber. A stylist will help you create a look that’s right for you—see him ever six weeks. A barber will help you maintain the look—see him every other week.”

6. Shampoo Your Hair Sparingly

Dan Revitte, Digital Creative Director

“Wash your hair with shampoo only three times a week. A little natural oil in the hair looks better, and healthier.”

“Three times a week is good—but always add a good conditioner to keep your hair soft.”

7. Add an Eye Cream To Your Grooming Routine

“Using moisturizer head to toe—SPF during the day and eye cream at night—it will make you look 40 when you’re 50.”

“The first signs of aging show up around your eyes, and an eye cream is one of the most efficient ways to protect your skin. Don’t just dab, either—make sure you’re applying a generous amount.”

We’re fans of Kiehl’s moisturizing avocado eye treatment ($48, kiehls.com).

8. Blot Excess Oil Off Your Face Regularly

Michael Sneeden, Video Producer “Every time I go to the bathroom now I dab my nose and forehead with a paper towel to make it less oily throughout the day. Game changer!”

“If you don’t have time to wash up, this is a great way to remove excess oil. Go for a tissue or toilet paper first, and paper towel if you’re out of options—but make sure to blot. Press the paper to your skin gently for a few seconds. Do. Not. Rub. The course texture of the paper can irritate the skin.”

9. There Is a Right and Wrong Way to Apply Cologne

“Don’t apply cologne directly to your body. Spray it in the air and walk through it.”

Shutterstock Salgardo’s verdict: Nay.

“My favorite way to apply is on both sides of the neck and wrists. Just never rub your wrists together—it muddles the scent.”

10. Don’t Double-Dip

Brian Boye, Executive Fashion and Grooming Director

“Don’t wash your face with the same soap you use on your ass.”

“A hand or body soap can be too harsh to use on your face. It strips your skin of its natural oil and can make it dry or itchy. Make sure you find a face cleanser with moisturizing oil in it.

“And FYI, if you use a soap that has deodorizing properties and try to wear cologne, the scent won’t stick. A deodorizing soap doesn’t know the difference between a good scent and a bad scent, so it kills all scents.”

Check out our list of the best shower gels for men, and give your face the respect it deserves with dedicated cleansing oil ($32, kiehls.com).
